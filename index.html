What Is a Neurodevelopmental Disorder (NDD)?

A Neurodevelopmental Disorder is a condition that affects how a child's brain grows and develops, showing up in early childhood and often lasting into adulthood. NDDs can affect attention, learning, communication, movement, and social skills — sometimes one of these areas, sometimes several at once.

Every child develops at their own pace, and having one or two of the traits below does not automatically mean a child has an NDD. But when several signs appear together and persist over time, an early conversation with a healthcare provider can make a real difference. Early identification generally leads to better outcomes, more effective support at school, and less frustration for both the child and the family.

Research on NDDs in Kenya

NDDs are more common — and more under-diagnosed — in Kenya than many parents realise. A study published in JAMA Network Open found that 522 of 11,223 children aged 6–9 screened across Kenyan sites were identified as having a Neurodevelopmental Disorder, yet the large majority had not received any clinical help or support before the study.

Types of NDDs and Common Symptoms

These are the conditions this site's screening tool can flag as a probable — not diagnosed — concern, based on the symptoms a parent selects.

ADHD Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder

Affects attention, impulse control, and activity levels.

  • Trouble paying attention or finishing tasks
  • Hyperactive, fidgety, or constantly moving
  • Acts before thinking; interrupts often
  • Easily distracted or forgetful

ASD Autism Spectrum Disorder

Affects social communication, interaction, and response to sensory input.

  • Limited eye contact or difficulty reading social cues
  • Strong need for routine; distress at change
  • Repetitive behaviours or intense focused interests
  • Sensory sensitivities (sound, light, texture)

Dyslexia Reading-Based Learning Disorder

Mainly affects reading and spelling, despite normal intelligence.

  • Slow, effortful reading
  • Confuses similar letters (e.g. b/d)
  • Struggles to sound out or spell words
  • Avoids reading aloud

Dysgraphia Writing-Based Learning Disorder

Affects handwriting and organising thoughts on paper.

  • Messy, slow, or laboured handwriting
  • Difficulty gripping a pencil correctly
  • Trouble copying written work
  • Written work doesn't match verbal ability

Dyscalculia Maths-Based Learning Disorder

Affects understanding of numbers and mathematical concepts.

  • Struggles with counting or basic arithmetic
  • Difficulty with times tables
  • Trouble telling time or understanding place value
  • Maths anxiety despite effort

Intellectual Disability

Involves limits in intellectual functioning and everyday adaptive skills.

  • Learning noticeably slower than peers, across subjects
  • Delayed developmental milestones
  • Difficulty with reasoning or understanding concepts
  • Needs support with daily life skills

Motor Disorder Developmental Coordination Disorder

Affects control of body movements and coordination.

  • Clumsy; frequent trips or falls
  • Poor balance for their age
  • Struggles with catching, running, or tying shoes
  • Difficulty with fine motor tasks (buttons, cutting)
